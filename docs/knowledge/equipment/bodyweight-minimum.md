# Summary

Bodyweight alone (`body only`[^fedb-schema]) covers most of what an early-teens keeper
needs: strength, jumping, short sprints and footwork. A soft surface is the one thing
bodyweight work cannot replace, because dives and repeated landings need it.

# What bodyweight covers

- Bodyweight exercises are an accepted mode of youth resistance training, and the NSCA
  advises starting with relatively simple exercises and light loads before progressing
  to harder multi-joint movements.[^nsca-2009]
- The AAP names simple hops and jumps as ways young people build strength.[^aap-2020-parents]
- Plyometric programmes, which need no equipment, improve running and jumping in
  youth.[^johnson-2011]
- WHO asks adolescents for muscle- and bone-strengthening activity at least three days a
  week; bodyweight work and jumping count.[^who-2020]
- Goalkeeper training is usually technical and explosive work in confined spaces, which
  fits a living room or yard.[^white-2018]

| Quality | Bodyweight options |
|---|---|
| Leg strength | Squats, split squats, lunges, single-leg balance squats, glute bridges |
| Upper body | Push-up progressions (wall, incline, floor), plank shoulder taps |
| Trunk | Planks, side planks, dead bugs, bird dogs |
| Jumping | Squat jumps, tuck jumps, lateral hops, skater bounds (on a forgiving surface) |
| Footwork and speed | Set-position drills, shuffles, crossover steps, 2 to 10 m (about 2 to 11 yd) starts where space allows |

# What bodyweight cannot cover well

- **Upper-body power for punching and throwing**: push-ups build strength, but fast
  throwing needs something to throw (a medicine ball or a football). See
  [home equipment](/equipment/home-equipment.md).
- **Progressive load** after bodyweight becomes easy: harder variants (single-leg, slower
  tempo, more range) help, but bands add a measured step.
- **Diving and repeated landings**: need grass or a mat. Without either, the app leaves
  dive drills out (see [substitutions](/equipment/substitutions.md)).
