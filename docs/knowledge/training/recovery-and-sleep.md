# Summary

Youth overuse injuries come from too much repetitive load with too little rest. Rest days,
variety and enough sleep are part of training, not breaks from it. Sources give slightly
different sleep ranges; the overlap is about 9 hours a night.

# Rest and total load

- The IOC links youth overuse injury, overtraining and burnout to heavy, repetitive loads
  and too little rest, especially when adolescents step up training levels too
  quickly.[^ioc-2015]
- The IOC asks for varied athletic exposure and regular rest and recovery, and says no
  youth athlete should train in a way that loads an injured area while in pain or not
  fully recovered from illness or injury.[^ioc-2015]
- The AAP clinical report on specialisation advises at least one to two days a week off
  from the main sport, longer breaks several times a year, and delaying single-sport
  specialisation until mid-to-late adolescence.[^brenner-2016]
- A rule of thumb sometimes quoted alongside that report, that weekly organised training
  hours should not exceed the athlete's age in years, comes from secondary commentary,
  not from a tested threshold. The app treats it as an upper bound, not a target.
- Strength sessions belong on non-consecutive days, two to three times a
  week.[^nsca-2009]
- **App implication:** home sessions add to team practice and matches. The app asks the
  parent for the weekly team schedule, never schedules on rest days, and keeps at least
  one, preferably two, full rest days a week.

# Sleep

| Source | Scope | Recommended sleep |
|---|---|---|
| IOC consensus (international) | Adolescent athletes | About 8.5 to 9.5 hours[^ioc-2015] |
| AASM consensus (United States) | Ages 13 to 18 | 8 to 10 hours in 24 hours[^paruthi-2016] |
| 24-Hour Movement Guidelines (Canada) | Ages 5 to 13 / 14 to 17 | 9 to 11 hours / 8 to 10 hours, with consistent bed and wake times[^csep-24h] |

- Adolescents naturally go to bed and wake later; early school starts, screens at night
  and caffeine shorten sleep, and preliminary evidence links insufficient sleep with more
  injuries in youth athletes.[^ioc-2015]
- The WHO asks adolescents to limit sedentary time, especially recreational screen
  time.[^who-2020]
- **Disagreement and conservative choice:** the ranges overlap at roughly 9 to 9.5 hours,
  and guidelines that split by age put early teens in different bands. The app uses about
  9 hours as the planning minimum and never schedules a session late enough to
  cut into it.
