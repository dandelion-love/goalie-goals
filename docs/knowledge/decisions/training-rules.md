# Decision

The app is a planner and a log, not a coach or a clinician. It prescribes only
exercises the owner (the parent) has approved, keeps technique before load, never
prescribes maximal lifts, stays inside the limits below, and stops a session and points
the family to a professional when a warning sign appears. Where sources disagree, the
app takes the more conservative option.

# Three non-negotiable rules

1. **Owner approval.** Every exercise, and every harder variant of it, starts as
   unapproved. The app adds nothing to a session that the owner has not approved. The
   owner can withdraw approval at any time and the app drops that exercise from future
   sessions. The owner is the supervisor the position statements require, and must be
   able to watch and correct each movement.[^nsca-2009][^lloyd-2014]
2. **Technique before load.** A new exercise starts with no added load (or the lightest
   band) for 1 to 2 sets of 10 to 15 repetitions. Load, speed or difficulty increase only
   after the owner records that every repetition was clean in two consecutive
   sessions.[^nsca-2009]
3. **No maximal lifts.** No one-repetition-maximum tests, no near-maximal sets, no
   repetitions to failure under load, no competitive or Olympic-style lifting. The NSCA
   and the 2014 Consensus allow supervised maximal testing by qualified professionals;
   the older AAP policy was more cautious; a home setting has no such professional, so
   the app takes the cautious side.[^nsca-2009][^lloyd-2014][^aap-2008]

# What the app may prescribe

- Dynamic warm-ups of 5 to 10 minutes and a light cool-down every session.[^nsca-2009]
- Bodyweight strength (squats, lunges, push-up variations, planks, bridges), elastic-band
  and light medicine-ball exercises, all major muscle groups.[^nsca-2009][^aap-2020-parents]
- Low-level plyometrics on a forgiving surface (jumps, hops, bounds, skater jumps) with
  landings taught first.[^johnson-2011]
- Short sprints and change-of-direction drills with full recovery.[^moran-2017-sprint]
- Goalkeeper technique: set position, footwork, catching, diving progressions onto grass
  or a thick mat, and punching with a soft ball. See
  [goalkeeper power](/training/goalkeeper-power.md).
- Mobility, balance and trunk work.

# What the app must never prescribe

- Maximal or near-maximal lifts or tests, or any set taken to failure under load.
- Barbell lifts, Olympic lifts and heavy free weights (they need a qualified coach on
  site).[^lloyd-2014]
- Depth or drop jumps from a height, weighted jumps, or jumping on concrete.
- Resisted sprints (sleds, harnesses, weighted vests).
- Improvised loads (a loaded backpack, a sibling, water containers) or equipment the
  household profile does not list (see [equipment model](/decisions/equipment-model.md)).
- Any exercise or variant the owner has not approved.
- Training through pain, or training an injured area before full recovery.[^ioc-2015]
- Supplements, caffeine or energy drinks, or any weight-loss, weight-gain or
  body-composition goal.[^sda-2014][^aap-2011-drinks][^reds-2023]

# Progression limits

| Lever | Limit | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Strength sessions | 2 to 3 a week, never on consecutive days | [^nsca-2009] |
| Sets and repetitions | Strength 1 to 3 sets of 6 to 15; power 1 to 3 sets of 3 to 6 | [^nsca-2009] |
| Load increase | At most 5% at a time (the low end of the NSCA's 5 to 10%), or one step of band or variant, after two clean sessions | [^nsca-2009] |
| Jump contacts | Start at 50 to 60 a session; never above about 100; at most twice a week | [^johnson-2011][^asadi-2017] |
| Sprints | Up to 16 efforts of about 20 m (22 yd) or less, at least 90 seconds rest, twice a week | [^moran-2017-sprint] |
| Rest | At least 1, preferably 2, full rest days a week, counting team training | [^brenner-2016] |
| Growth spurt | When the owner reports fast growth or new knee or heel pain, hold all progressions and drop jump and sprint volume back to the starting dose | [^ioc-2015][^moran-2017-cmj] |
| After a break | After a week or more off, restart one step below the last level | Cautious reading of [^nsca-2009] |

# Warning signs that stop a session

The app shows a stop button on every screen. The session ends at once, and the app
records why, when the keeper or the owner reports any of these:

| Sign | What the app says next |
|---|---|
| Chest pain, fainting or near-fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or unusual shortness of breath during or after exercise | Stop. Call the local emergency number if the keeper collapses or the symptom is severe; otherwise see a doctor before any more training.[^aap-scd] |
| A knock to the head followed by any symptom (headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, blurred vision, feeling "not right") | Stop. No more activity that day. See a health professional before returning.[^concussion-2023] |
| Pain in a joint, bone or growth area (below the kneecap, heel, lower back), or pain that changes how the keeper moves | Stop. No training of that area until it is pain-free; see a professional if it lasts or returns.[^ioc-2015] |
| Swelling, a pop, giving way, or limping | Stop. See a professional before training that area again. |
| Signs of heat illness (headache, nausea, confusion, unusual tiredness in heat) | Stop, cool down, drink water; seek medical help if not quickly better.[^ioc-2015] |
| Illness or fever | Do not start or continue; evaluate before resuming.[^aap-2020-parents] |
| Technique breaking down from fatigue (loud landings, knees caving, arching back) | End that set, rest, and finish the session with easier work or stop. |

# When to see a professional

- **Before starting** if the keeper has a known heart condition, uncontrolled high blood
  pressure, a seizure disorder, a history of chemotherapy, or a family history of
  sudden unexplained death at a young age.[^aap-2020][^aap-2020-parents][^aap-scd]
- **After any stop sign** in the table above that involves the heart, the head, or pain
  that persists or returns.[^aap-scd][^concussion-2023][^ioc-2015]
- **For food and weight:** signs of under-eating, dieting, or worry about weight or body
  shape point to a doctor or registered dietitian, not to the app.[^reds-2023]
- **To go beyond these limits:** any heavier lifting, barbell work, maximal testing or
  resisted sprinting needs a qualified strength and conditioning professional in person;
  the app does not plan it.[^lloyd-2014]

# Disagreements resolved conservatively

| Topic | Disagreement | App choice |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength testing | Allowed under qualified supervision (NSCA, 2014 Consensus) vs more cautious (AAP 2008) | Never |
| Load increments | 5 to 10% (NSCA) | 5% or one variant step |
| Jump volume | 50 to 60 contacts to start (Johnson) vs about 100 a session (Asadi) | Start at 50 to 60, cap at about 100 |
| Sleep | 8.5 to 9.5 h (IOC), 8 to 10 h (AASM), 9 to 11 or 8 to 10 h by age band (Canadian guideline) | Plan for at least about 9 h |
| Caffeine | 2.5 mg/kg (Health Canada), about 3 mg/kg (EFSA), none (AAP) | None |
| Weekly hours | "No more hours than age" is commentary, not a tested threshold | Upper bound only, plus rest days |

# Consequences

- The exercise library carries an approval state per exercise and per variant, owned by
  the parent profile.
- Each session log records clean-repetition checks, any stop sign, and growth notes, which
  drive the progression rules above.
