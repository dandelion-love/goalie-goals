# What a themed-day rotation is

Many home training programmes, in books, apps and videos, give each weekday a fixed
focus and repeat the same pattern every week. The focus is usually one of:

- a **body region** (lower body, upper body, trunk, whole body);
- a **quality** (strength, power, conditioning, mobility, skill);
- **recovery** (a gentle movement day, or a named rest day).

The pattern is published as a weekly grid, the theme often carries a short catchy name,
and the rest day sits in a fixed place. This page describes the general idea only; it
names no programme and copies no names, day labels, schedules or exercises.

# Why it works

- **It removes a daily decision.** The weekday answers "what are we doing today?", which
  is the cue-to-action link habit research describes.[^wood-2007] It is a weekly
  if-then plan: "on this day, this theme" (see
  [stable cues](/progression-library/stable-cues-and-habit.md)).
- **It spaces similar work.** Putting hard strength days apart, with different themes in
  between, gives the recovery the youth guidance asks for: strength on 2 to 3
  non-consecutive days.[^nsca-2009]
- **It meets the activity guidance in pieces.** Adolescents are advised to do
  muscle- and bone-strengthening activity on at least 3 days a week, inside daily
  activity;[^who-2020] a rotation spreads that across the week.
- **It gives predictable variety.** Each day differs from the last, but nothing is
  random, so the pair can plan around it.
- **It tolerates a miss.** A missed day is one cell in a grid that comes round again next
  week, and one miss does not undo a habit.[^lally-2010]

# Where it goes wrong

| Risk | Why it matters for an early-teens keeper |
|---|---|
| Every day becomes a hard day | Themes that each look "light" can add up to daily high load; youth guidance asks for at least 1 to 2 days a week off.[^brenner-2016] |
| Adult volumes and intensities | Many programmes are written for adults and use sets to failure or maximal effort, which the [training rules](/decisions/training-rules.md) refuse.[^training-rules] |
| Rigid against real life | A fixed grid ignores team training and matches; jumps the day before a match, or strength the day after, stack load. |
| Jump and sprint stacking | Two themes that both include jumping can break the twice-a-week jump limit without anyone noticing.[^training-rules] |
| Copying | Programme names, day labels and routines are someone's work; the app uses its own names and its own approved exercises. |

# What this app borrows

1. **The same theme on the same weekday**, chosen by the parent, so the weekday is the
   cue.
2. **Hard days apart, light days between**, with light days defined by caps on time and
   intensity so they stay light (see the [proposed week](/progression-library/proposed-week.md)).
3. **A named rest day** that is part of the plan, never a gap.
4. **Team training and matches as fixed points** the rotation bends around.
5. **Our own theme names and only owner-approved exercises.**

What it leaves out: adult doses, sets to failure, challenge-style escalation inside a
week, and any programme's names or content.
