# Summary

The library covers sixteen ladders in four groups. Each pattern is in because a
goalkeeper uses it in a save, a landing or getting up again, and because youth guidance
names it: the NSCA asks for all major muscle groups and simple exercises first,[^nsca-2009]
the NSCA's long-term development statement puts movement skill before load,[^nsca-ltad-2016]
and the FIFA 11+ programme, tested with teenage players, already arranges planks, side
planks, squats, single-leg work and jumps in three levels of difficulty.[^soligard-2008][^bizzini-2015]
The goalkeeper actions follow the position's demands: few, explosive saves, jumps and
quick lateral steps.[^gk-power]

"Default profile" is the generic "bodyweight and home" profile of the equipment model:
`body only`, a ball, a wall and a step, on a hard floor.[^equipment-model] A rung that
needs more is left out until the owner adds the item or place; it is never replaced by an
improvised object.[^substitutions]

# What the patterns leave out

- **Sprints over about 10 m.** The speed research favours 2 to 10 m starts, shuffles and
  dives for a keeper,[^speed] so speed sits inside the lateral-movement and footwork
  ladders, and full sprints stay with the existing sprint limits.
- **Hamstring lowering (Nordic curls).** FIFA 11+ includes them,[^soligard-2008] but they
  need a partner pinning the ankles and are a hard eccentric effort; the library leaves
  them out at home, and any later rung needs the owner's decision.
- **Repeated or depth jumps.** Excluded by the plyometric rules; jumping ladders stop at
  single jumps landed and frozen.[^plyo]
