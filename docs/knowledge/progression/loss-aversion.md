# Science

- People weigh a loss more heavily than a gain of the same size, relative to a reference
  point.[^kt]
- The reference app says plainly that long streaks keep people practising because they do
  not want to lose them.[^duo-streak-research]
- People will incur costs to repair a broken logged streak, and broken streaks reduce later
  engagement, more so when people blame themselves.[^silverman]
- Slack that prevents a single miss from counting as failure keeps people going.[^sharif]

# Risks (AC4)

Loss aversion is the lever most likely to turn a habit into an obligation: training while
sore or ill "to save the streak", or a parent pressing a tired teen. It is also the lever
behind paid streak repair. For a young athlete, a mechanic that makes skipping feel like
a loss directly competes with the rule to rest and to stop on pain.

# Takeaways

Use gain framing (what you built) rather than loss framing (what you will lose). Make rest
and safety stops non-events for every counter. See
[progression design](/decisions/progression-design.md).
