# Summary

Each country got the same three checks: its licence page,[^licences] its defence-force
fitness preparation guide, and its national health agency's exercise guidance. Only US
federal works yielded figures that can enter the app, because US law leaves federal
works without copyright; the other countries' open licences pass, but the exercise
figures checked were photographs, excluded images, "all rights reserved" guides, or
pages that could not be read. The two usable US works are old line-drawing manuals
written for adult service members: their pictures may be adapted, their doses never.

# What may be taken from the usable works

- The drawing only, redrawn in the app's style: own palette, own body shapes, no
  uniform, no insignia, no rank or unit marks, no equipment the app does not use.
- Not the exercise names' sets, repetitions, cadence counts, rest, progressions,
  programmes or test standards. The app's doses come only from the training-rules
  Decision.
- Not a figure whose page or caption names another author, book or company.

# Gaps

- FM 21-20's figures were not seen as images; a person confirms each one is a drawing
  before it is used.
- The UK and Australian defence guides, CDC Growing Stronger and the Australian health
  PDFs could not be read on 2026-11-18, and the New Zealand defence pages were not
  checked. Each is worth one more try.
- Only the national level was checked in each country; state, provincial and regional
  health services publish more exercise sheets, under their own terms.
