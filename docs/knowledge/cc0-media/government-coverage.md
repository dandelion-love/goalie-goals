# Summary

Government and health-body sources do not cover the app's exercises. Of the 9 cards and
13 goalkeeper moves (22 rows), 3 have a public-domain line drawing of a close variant
that could be redrawn as a starting pose; none has an attribution-only candidate; 19
have none found. Every drawing that passes comes from one 1997 US Navy guide, and each
is a near variant, not the exercise as the card teaches it. The goalkeeper moves have no
government figure at all. So the app's own parametric figure stays the main route, as
the media Decision already says, and these drawings serve at most as pose references.

# How to read the table

- **Licence class**: PD (public domain, US federal work by statute) or BY (open licence
  that needs a credit line). No row is BY: the open licences pass, but no figure under
  them did.[^licences]
- **Style fit**: good (a clean side view the app's flat figure can follow), fair (usable
  pose, but a different variant or an awkward angle), poor.
- Page numbers are the PDF's page index in the copy cited.
- "Line drawing" for the Navy guide is read from the file: each figure is a small
  one-bit image beside its exercise, which is how line art is stored. A person confirms
  each by eye before it is redrawn.
- Every figure is taken as a drawing to redraw, never with its dose, cadence or
  programme, and never with uniform or insignia.[^training-rules]
