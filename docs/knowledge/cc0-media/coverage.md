# How to read the table

- **Rows:** the nine cards in `content/exercises/`, then the goalkeeper moves named in the training research.[^cards][^gk-power][^speed] Planks and side planks appear once, as cards.
- **Candidate:** the best file found whose own page states CC0 or public domain. "Partial" means it shows part of the move or a close variant. Thumbnails were checked by eye on 2026-11-18; poses were not measured.
- **Style fit:** how close the file is to the app's flat, rounded figure as it stands. **Adapt:** how easily it becomes a pose for that figure (trace the joint angles, then draw in the app's style).
- **AI:** "no" means no AI flag on the page and an upload before 2022; Commons files show no AI category.[^openclipart][^commons]
- Some Openclipart files are traced in a period style (tags such as 1917 or 1919) from an unnamed older image. An outline or silhouette of that kind is listed as a pose reference only; a traced figure that could identify a real person is not listed.[^openclipart]
- Files that only Public Domain Vectors or FreeSVG offer are not listed, since those sites fail the licence check.[^pdv] Character and icon sets are covered on their own page.[^sets]

# What this means

- CC0 drawings cover only the most generic moves (push-up, planks) and the most iconic keeper poses (the dive and the high catch), and almost every one needs re-posing to match a card.
- Every candidate is better used as a pose reference for the app's own figure than shipped as is; the styles differ file to file.
- The gaps (bridge, shuffle, split squat, set stance, kneeling and crouch dives, medicine-ball and band work, the dead bug) are drawn by the app, as the exercise-media decision already says.
